# UX Heuristics

What counts as friction. One rule per heuristic; check each at every step of
the walkthrough. The skill's completion criteria demand every heuristic be
applied — a friction point missed here is a finding lost.

## Navigation
- Can the user tell where they are, where they came from, and where they can
  go next?
- Is the path back to a known state (home, cancel) always one tap away?

## Visual hierarchy
- Does the eye land on the right element first — the primary action, not
  chrome?
- Is anything competing for attention that should defer to the primary
  action?

## Empty states
- When there is no data, does the screen say what to do next?
- Is the empty state an invitation to act?

## Error states
- Does an error name what went wrong and what the user can do about it?
- Is the error blame-free in tone?

## Tone
- Is the language calm and plain?
- Does the screen feel intimidating — too many options, too much density, too
  much assumed knowledge? (This is the "enjoyable and unintimidating" test
  from EOM-S16.)

## Affordances
- Can the user tell what is interactive and what is not?
- Do controls signal their effect before they are used?

## Flow continuity
- Is the user carried from one step to the next, or dropped between them?
- Is state preserved across the transitions that matter?

## Cognitive load
- How much must the user hold in their head at once to take the next step?
- Could a step be removed, merged, or deferred without losing the user?
